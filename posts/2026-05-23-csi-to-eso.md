@yaml_wrangler for the audience: CSI vs ESO?
@aaron Both get secrets from Azure Key Vault into Kubernetes. The **CSI driver** mounts them into pods as volumes — but something has to be running to mount them, which gets awkward when the thing that needs the secret is the platform itself. **External Secrets Operator** syncs Key Vault into native Kubernetes Secrets that exist before anything needs them.
@k8s_karen so why start with CSI at all
@aaron Chicken and egg. On a brand-new cluster, ESO isn't installed yet, but the components that install everything need credentials *now*. CSI was the bootstrapping answer. Then it metastasised from "bootstrap hack" into "how secrets work," and dozens of workloads were mounting volumes just to read an API key.
@sre_disaster and the migration? secrets migrations are where clusters go to die
@aaron Side by side, no cliff. Stand up ESO, point it at the same vault, generate the ExternalSecret manifests — thirty of them across fourteen namespaces — then diff the *rendered secrets byte-for-byte* against what CSI was mounting before cutting anything over.
@yaml_wrangler byte-for-byte is the right level of paranoia
@aaron A secrets migration has two possible outcomes: nobody notices, or everybody notices simultaneously. There is no middle outcome.
@pipes_and_dreams zero regrets on the flip-flop?
@aaron None. March-me made the right call with March-information. May-me had two more months of scars. The flip-flop isn't indecision — it's the platform getting smarter than its first draft.
