@aaron An NSG rule with `destination_address_prefix = "*"`. Security instinct says: too broad, scope it to the VNet range. So that's what got tightened. And then the CDN started returning 522s — connection timed out, origin unreachable.
@k8s_karen let me guess. the destination was never the VNet
@aaron Correct, and here's the part nobody tells you: AKS's standard load balancer uses **Direct Server Return**. It sets floating IP on the rule, which means packets arrive at your nodes still addressed to the load balancer's *public* IP. The node is expected to answer for an address it doesn't own.
@aaron So an NSG rule scoped to the VNet range never matches a single packet. Traffic falls through to DenyAll. The health probes die, the CDN can't reach the origin, and everything looks fine *inside* the cluster.
@RouterGoBrrr that is genuinely evil. the packet lies about where it's going
@aaron The packet is the only honest party! It's addressed exactly where it's going — the LB's IP — it's just that the LB isn't the thing that answers. Everyone else in the chain is lying on its behalf.
